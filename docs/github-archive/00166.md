Tapping a building at street level opened nothing, so the eye-level picking work looked broken in its first test. The camera ray did find the building. Two later steps threw the result away.

## What changes

- The tap finds its block by testing which block polygon holds the ray's hit point (`blockContaining` in `src/lib/geo/blocks.ts`). The old lookup used the 0.003° grid key, but real blocks are tax blocks keyed by their own ids, so it never matched. Overhead clicks that fall back to a ground point use the same lookup now.
- `MapPanel` stops hiding itself for the whole walk. At street level it opens when a tap there picked something (a block, a landmark, a storefront). A selection carried in from overhead still stays shut when you enter street level.
- The eye-level picking from the old `feat/eye-level-picking` working tree comes along as it was: `eyePick.ts`, its tests, the tap branch in `MapView`, and `storefrontAt` in `threeLayer.ts`. No three.js outside `threeLayer.ts`.

## How to verify

Walk to Park Av S at E 21 St (`__walk({ lon: -73.98795, lat: 40.73905 })`), turn to bearing 300 and tap the storefront facade at screen centre. The panel opens on "Manhattan block 850 · East 22 Street / East 21 Street" with 22 noise complaints. Screenshots from a headless Chrome DevTools Protocol run on the box: `nyc-map-qa/eyetap-before.png` (no panel) and `nyc-map-qa/eyetap-after-panel.png` (panel open).

Local checks before push: typecheck, lint, dependency-cruiser and the unit tests all pass (139 tests, 8 skipped as before), and the all-JS bundle is 919 kB against the 925 kB budget. The Project Continuity Modules (PCM) preflight at commit 70dc17d reports TARGET_VALID, and Content Generation Modules (CGM) at commit 100f3c3 validates the helper and this repo's adapter.

## What stays the same

Overhead clicks, hover and the feature layers' own click handlers behave as before. The street-level header and walk controls are unchanged.
