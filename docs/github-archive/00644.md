Subway trains on the live map sit in stations far longer than a real train stops, because the vehicle feed's "stopped at" report pinned a train in place with no time limit.

## Human outcome

Trains on the map move like real trains: they stop for about 30 seconds to a minute and then roll on, unless the line really is in trouble.

## Before and after

**Before:** on a live sample of 480 trips, the timetable model has 12% of trains parked, and the pin rule held another 22%, so 34% were drawn parked. The pin lasted until the next vehicle report, which is a median 29 seconds and up to 71 seconds old.

**After:** the report holds a train at a station for at most 60 seconds after its own timestamp (`MAX_PIN_S`). After that the timetable's motion is used. The limit does not apply when the line is in trouble: the held-train detector flags the train, or the route has an active delay or detour alert.

## Change and scope

- `src/features/transit/model.ts`: `MAX_PIN_S` and the `troubled` option of `estimateTrain`.
- `src/features/transit/TransitLayer.tsx`: passes `troubled` for each train, from the existing alert and held-train data.
- `src/features/transit/maxHold.test.ts`: a fresh report, a stale report, a troubled line, and a train the timetable already has at the station.

## What stays unchanged

Default dwell lengths, the held-train detector, buses, and the line panel.
