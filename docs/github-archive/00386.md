MTA disruption notices are hard to decode: they don't show which stops are skipped, where shuttle buses run, where to switch direction, which train to take instead, or when a train is stuck.

## What changes
- `src/lib/transit/trouble.ts` (pure, tested against 7 recorded real alerts and live train samples) derives, from MTA data only:
  - where the problem is (station named in live delay headlines);
  - skipped stops (from the headline; the alert's stop list is cut to the "between A and B" stretch, minus stations the text says still have trains; a first version got a live G alert wrong and is fixed);
  - shuttle buses with the MTA's own boarding-corner wording, and where to get the train back ("Transfer … at X");
  - where to switch direction, only from MTA sentences (no dataset lists crossovers, so nothing else is computed);
  - which train instead (other lines at a skipped station's complex; daytime lists, so nights and weekends are approximate);
  - a stuck train: no position update for 5+ minutes mid-trip on a line with a live delay alert, shown at its station (the feed has no coordinates). A live check matched a Q at Canal St during a fire delay.
- `line_status` returns all of this with a new `showTrouble` answer button. The prompt gains a rule: explain in plain words and never suggest a stop or switch station the data didn't give.
