**Problem:** the recommended entrance or exit was simply the one closest to your destination in a straight line. That could send you to an elevator-only exit whose elevator was out, with only a warning attached. A straight line also ignores avenues and blocks, so a slightly closer exit could mean a longer walk.

**What changes** (`portalFor` in `server/features/agent/trip.ts`)
- **Broken elevators are skipped.** An elevator-only exit is skipped while the station reports an elevator outage and another way out exists. The MTA's outage list names the station, not the entrance, so any elevator outage at the station counts. If the elevator is the only way out, it's still chosen, with the outage as its warning, which now quotes only elevator outages (not escalators).
- **The shortest walk wins.** The three exits closest in a straight line are compared by real walking distance from the same walking router the app already uses, and the shortest walk wins. If that router is down, the straight-line choice is kept.
- The same rule applies to entrances, measured from where you start.

**How to verify:** 4 new unit tests in `server/features/agent/portal.test.ts`: shortest walk beats shortest straight line, a broken-elevator exit is skipped, an elevator that's the only way out keeps its warning, and a router outage falls back to the straight line.

**What stays unchanged:** how exits are named ("NE exit on Lexington Av"), the trip plan itself, and the exit advice text.
