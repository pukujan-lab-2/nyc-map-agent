On the VPS the ferry layer is empty: `GET /api/v1/ferry` returns 502, because it asks an OpenTripPlanner (OTP) that only ever ran on gravebuster.

## Human outcome

Riders see NYC Ferry and Staten Island Ferry routes, piers and boats on the live map again. CI's live contract check can then be turned on (#614), since this endpoint was its only server error.

## Before and after

**Before:** the route posted a GraphQL query to `http://nyc-map-otp:8080`. Nothing answers there on the VPS, so every call ended in `TypeError: fetch failed` and a 502.

**After:** the route downloads the two ferry GTFS feeds from their publishers, about 50 KB and 10 KB zipped, and caches them for 6 hours. It adds NYC Ferry's live vehicle positions every 30 seconds. Against the real feeds on 2026-11-01 it returned 200 with 8 routes (the six NYC Ferry lines, St. George and the Staten Island Ferry), 44 terminals, and 2 Staten Island boats placed by the schedule at 10:37 pm.

## Change and scope

- `server/features/ferry/gtfs.ts` (new): a small CSV reader, building routes, shapes, stops, trips and service calendars from one feed. Ids are prefixed with the feed name so the two feeds cannot collide.
- `server/features/ferry/routes.ts`: `toFerryData` takes that timetable and optional live boats. Scheduled boats come from today's and yesterday's service days, so trips after midnight ("25:10:00") still show, and a service day starts at New York midnight across daylight saving time. Live boats replace a route's schedule estimates unless they are older than 10 minutes, as before.
- `package.json`: adds `fflate` 0.8.2 to unzip the feeds.
- `docs/OTP.md`: one paragraph saying the ferry layer no longer uses OTP.
- Checkpoint files, and `tasks/TASK-NMA-0002-ferry-without-otp.md` for the continuity checkpoint.

## What stays unchanged

The response shape (`routes`, `terminals`, `vessels`), so the map's ferry layer and `openapi.json` need no change. The trip planner (MOTIS on the VPS), and the OTP deploy files kept for other hosts.
