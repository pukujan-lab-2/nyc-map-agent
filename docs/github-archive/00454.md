Live View (Beta) showed the phone camera with a flat arrow on top, so you still had to work out which street the arrow meant. Now the walking route is drawn onto the street in the picture, the way Google Maps Live View does it.

Refs #192

## What changes

- **`ArOverlay.tsx` (new).** A second MapLibre map, with no basemap and a clear background, sits over the camera picture. It holds only the navigation layers the map already uses: the glowing route, chevrons and the turn arrow. It is posed as an eye 1.4 m above the GPS fix, looking along the smoothed compass heading and tipped 8° toward the pavement, so the ribbon lies on the real street.
- **`LiveViewBeta.tsx`.** It shows the overlay when the camera, the compass and a fix within 25 m are all available. Otherwise it keeps the big 2D arrow toward the next turn, as before. The instruction line, the accuracy line and "Stop camera" are unchanged.
- **`RouteGuide.tsx`** passes the route, the distance walked and the GPS fix through. `NavFrame` in `layers.ts` is exported for that.
- **A way in from the home screen.** The home walking card ("Turn left · In 60 m") had no Live View button, so the camera view was only reachable from the older map layout. The card now has a camera button beside stop. Opening it starts GPS for as long as the camera is up, and closing it stops GPS again unless navigation is running. The full-screen view is drawn into the page body, so the card's blur effect can't trap it inside the card.
- The flag label now reads "Live View (Beta): route drawn over the camera".

## How it was checked

- Typecheck, lint, the module-boundary check and every unit test pass (1235 tests). New tests cover the icon trigger and check that nothing asks for the camera before the tap.
- In a phone-sized browser with a fake camera, a fake GPS fix 10 m accurate and a fake compass, the full home flow works: share location, Restroom, Walk me there, the camera button on the walking card, then Live View. The blue walking route is drawn in perspective over the camera picture, "Turn left, 60 m" shows, and the flat arrow is hidden.
- On a 375-pixel-wide screen the walking card still fits: the camera and stop buttons sit side by side, the turn text wraps to two lines, and the walker's dot stays on screen.

## What stays the same

Live View stays behind the `liveviewAr` flag, which is off by default. Nothing is recorded or uploaded: the camera picture never reaches the map, and the overlay fetches nothing.

## Still open on #192

How well the ribbon lines up depends on real compass and GPS error on a phone outdoors. The test here used a fake camera and a fixed fake compass.
