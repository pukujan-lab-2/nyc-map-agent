Stations have no signal, people lose their sense of direction underground and don't know which exit to take, and maps assume they're on the street.

## What changes (behind the new flag `underground`, off by default)
- A hand-written service worker (`public/sw.js`, no new dependency) keeps the app shell and static station data; it never caches `/api/*`.
- When a subway trip is on screen, each station you'll get off at is saved on the phone (exits, corner names, destination; about 2.5 kB) plus nearby map tiles.
- New `public/transit/equipment.json` (29 kB, from `scripts/build-equipment.ts`): the 164 MTA elevator and escalator units whose text names a street corner. Stairs get approximate corner names; outages show with their age.
- Flow, all on the phone: a one-time "Are you in the station?" (never again after a No); stops counted from phone motion with "Did you get off at…?" and Undo; an optional 5-second "point the way the train went" calibration with an approximate pointer and a stay-behind-the-yellow-line safety line; on surfacing, a landmark or corner check and direction from your first steps. GPS is never used for position underground, and nothing is tracked or sent.

## How to verify
46 new tests, typecheck, lint and depcruise pass. In the browser the exit view showed "Exit · E 42 St & Lexington Av · SE corner", and browser storage held the saved station and map tile.

## Not checked
The service worker would not register in the browser pane, so offline loading is unverified; the pane couldn't be switched offline; no compass or motion sensor on a desktop; no trip could be planned without the backend. Motion-based stop counting is approximate and works only with the screen on. Saving map tiles costs about **712 kB at Grand Central** (the basemap's most detailed tiles are large): consider saving tiles only for the destination station.
