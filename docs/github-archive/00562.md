**Problem.** People coming up from the subway often don't know which way they're facing, and phone compasses are often off by a steady amount in the city. The cone on your marker always looked equally sure, even when it was guessing.

**What changes**
- **Street lock**, in the shared heading code (`src/lib/compass/heading.ts`). After 4 steady walking samples, the gap between the compass and the walking direction becomes a correction. Train rides and vague fixes don't teach it.
- **Stair facing** (`src/lib/compass/surfacing.ts`, new). On the first sharp fix beside a stair after a spell below ground, you face that stair's way up. This also corrects the compass. If the subway file has no mapped way up, it uses the direction away from the station.
- **Cone width** (`src/lib/map/youLayer.ts`). There are three cones: narrow for a corrected compass, middle for a plain compass, walking or a stair, and wide while the compass needs calibrating.

**How to verify**
- Unit tests: `src/lib/compass/heading.test.ts` and `surfacing.test.ts` (215 tests in compass and map pass).
- e2e: `e2e/you-marker.spec.ts` checks that walking in `?sim=walk` gives a heading with the middle cone and that all three cone images are installed.

**What stays unchanged.** Camera modes, the dot, the accuracy circle, and the underground helper's own compass.
