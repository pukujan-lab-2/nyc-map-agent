## Problem

When a subway train is stuck, the map does not say so. A rider watching their train sees it keep gliding along on schedule, or sitting still with no explanation. The live MTA feed (GTFS-realtime, the MTA's machine-readable stream of trips and vehicle positions) already carries what we need, but the server threw most of it away:

- **Cancelled trips** still appeared on the map as running trains.
- **Stops the feed marks as skipped** stayed in the trip, so a train paused at a station it was passing straight through, and the station's arrival list showed it.
- **The vehicle's own report** of where it is ("stopped at Bay Pkwy") was ignored, so once the predicted time passed, the map drew the train beyond a station it had not left.

That works against the project's primary job (PROJECT.md, goal 5): "tell you when a train is stuck and exactly where".

## What changes

1. **Server, `slimTrips`** (`server/features/transit/routes.ts`): cancelled trips are dropped; skipped stops are taken out of the trip's path and listed separately; the vehicle's status (stopped at, or on its way to, a stop) and the time of its last position are kept. This is a pure function, tested against real feed data captured from the MTA on 2026-10-21.
2. **Hold rule, shared** (`src/lib/transit/holds.ts`): ported from #198. A train counts as held when its next arrival time keeps slipping later while it reports the same stop, or when it has reported the same stop for 3 minutes or more after its predicted time. A train at its first or last stop is never held, because trains wait there by design. A hold is shown only after it has lasted 3 minutes, so a short wait at a signal is not reported. The map layer and the line panel read the same verdicts.
3. **Train position** (`estimateTrain` in `model.ts`): a train is never drawn past the stop the vehicle still reports. If the vehicle says it is stopped there, the map shows it stopped at that platform.
4. **On the map**: a held train shows "Held 4 min" in amber beside its line bullet.
5. **In the line panel**: each held train on the line is a notice at the top of the list, for example "F train held at Bay Pkwy · To Coney Island-Stillwell Av · 5 min". Tapping it rings that station on the map, the same way other notices do. No new panels or screens.
6. **Bundle size**: the transit layers chunk had 21 bytes of headroom, and this change added about 0.9 kB. Instead of raising that budget, the line panel now loads as its own chunk the first time a line is picked. That puts the transit layers chunk at 20.8 kB (budget 23 kB). The new chunk has its own budget: `feature: line panel (lazy)`, 4.3 kB, currently 4.16 kB.

## What stays the same

- MTA alert notices, the skipped-station marks drawn from alerts, reroute drawing (`alertFocus.ts`, `lineNotices.ts`) and the agent's explanation of MTA trouble (`src/lib/transit/trouble.ts`, `server/features/agent/`) are unchanged. Main already covers those, in its own way.
- How trains move between updates, and the rule that they never jump backwards, are unchanged.

## Left out of #198, and why

- **Plain-English alert summaries and placing alerts on the map** (`summarizeAlert`, `alertSegments`, `parseBetween`): main already does this through `trouble.ts`, `serviceChanges.ts` and `alertFocus.ts`. The captured alerts fixture (about 2,000 lines) was only needed for those tests, so it is not added.
- **Rerouted trains riding another line's track** (`trackFor`, `tripPath`): main's movement model follows one route shape per trip and tracks progress along it. Switching shapes in the middle of a trip needs its own change to that model.
- **The old interface** (cards, layers drawer, themes, compass): replaced by the agent-first home in #395.

## Open questions

- The agent's "stuck train" rule (`stuckTrains`: no position update for 5 minutes on a line with a delay alert) and the map's hold rule are different tests. Whether the agent should use `holds.ts` too is a follow-up.
- Seen while porting, not changed here: the server reads a missing `direction_id` as 0, so every subway trip gets `dir: 0`. Fixing it would change the keys that segment-history lookups use, so it needs its own change.
