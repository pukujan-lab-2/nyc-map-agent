**Problem:** in street view every subway stair pointed at the middle of its station, so most stood sideways to the sidewalk, and the steps looked like slabs floating over the ground. Coming up out of the subway, nothing told you which way you were facing, which is when GPS and the compass are least reliable.

**What changes**
- Each stair takes its real way up from OpenStreetMap's mapped steps (316 of 2,120 entrances, baked by the new `pnpm build:entrance-stairs`). Without mapped steps, it lies along the nearest curb, going down toward the station's side (`src/lib/map/stairs.ts`).
- A dark stairwell (walls and floor) under each opening, so the steps read as going down.
- The exit helper: after "I'm out", when the exit's stairs are mapped, it asks "Came up the stairs at …? Then you're facing north. Turn about 90° to your right." One tap turns the map to match. Unmapped stairs keep the existing landmark and corner questions, so it never guesses a direction that could be backwards.

**How to verify:** in street view near 57 St (F) or DeKalb Av, the stairs run along the sidewalk. Unit tests cover the curb fallback, the OSM direction reading, and exits carrying `up` for stairs only.

**Unchanged:** entrance positions and kinds; the subway.json diff is only the new `up` field (checked: identical otherwise).
