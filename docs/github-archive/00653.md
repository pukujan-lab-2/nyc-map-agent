Trips on the live site come from MOTIS and walking routes from a free public OSRM server, and the owner chose OpenTripPlanner (OTP) for trips, because step-free routing and LIRR trips are coming, and a Valhalla we run ourselves for walks.

## Human outcome

The chat plans trips with OTP and draws walking routes from our own Valhalla on the VPS. Walks keep working when the public server is slow or down, and step-free trips can be built on top next.

## Before and after

**Before:** `TRIP_PLANNER=motis` on the VPS. Walking routes went to routing.openstreetmap.de, which promises no service level.

**After:** the VPS runs `nyc-map-otp` and `nyc-map-valhalla` next to the app. The app plans with OTP. Walks come from Valhalla, and the public OSRM server is used only when Valhalla fails. MOTIS keeps running until 2026-11-08 so `TRIP_PLANNER=motis` can switch back.

I ran 14 trips through both planners for Monday 2 November at 8:30, using the app's own clients and ranking. OTP's best trip had fewer changes in 7 trips and the same number in the other 5. It arrived earlier in 4, at the same time in 1, and 3 to 9 minutes later in 7. MOTIS often saves those minutes by adding a third change or a last bus. OTP also found Flushing to JFK, where MOTIS found nothing, though its answer is a slow 109-minute bus trip. Neither found Penn Station to Hicksville; see the caveat below.

## Change and scope

- `deploy/docker-compose.vps.yml`: the `otp` and `valhalla` services. The app gets `OTP_URL` and `VALHALLA_URL`, and `TRIP_PLANNER: motis` is gone.
- `deploy/otp/build-graph.sh`: VPS paths. The build container no longer gets the app's env file. It writes `otp.env` with only `MTA_BUSTIME_KEY`, the one secret the server reads (#595 already kept the rest out of the server).
- `deploy/valhalla/build.sh`: builds the tiles in a one-shot container. The serving container never builds.
- `server/features/agent/valhalla.ts`: the walking client. Turns onto unnamed walkways are dropped, so crossing a plaza doesn't produce five "turn left onto the walkway" steps.
- `server/features/agent/services.ts`: `routeWalk` tries Valhalla first.
- `server/features/agent/polyline.ts`: the polyline decoder, moved out of `otp.ts` so the new module doesn't import `otp.ts` in a loop.
- `deploy/README.md`: the containers, both rebuilds and the switch back.

## What stays unchanged

The trip tool, the ranking, the Trip shape the map draws, and the subway-only fallback router. The browser's street-view steps still call the public OSRM server. Step-free trip search is the next pull request: OTP's wheelchair option plus Valhalla's wheelchair profile.

## Verification

- `npx vitest run server`: 440 tests pass, including 4 new Valhalla tests on a real response from the VPS. `tsc` and `eslint` are clean.
- The graph and tiles are already built on the VPS: the OTP graph took 4 minutes to build and is 440 MB, and the Valhalla tiles took 1 minute. Trial containers served the comparison above and were then removed. OTP started in under a minute and used 2.8 GB of memory.
