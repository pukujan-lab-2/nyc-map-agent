Details

The screens
  • Today's phone screen is a real capture of the app's restroom answer at 390×844, on the blank test map the screen tests use. Today's card comes from the desktop street-level view.
  • The proposed results keep the app's rules: one short line and one main action ("Walk me there"); details and sources behind More; the restroom pictogram, never "WC".
  • Filters (Open now, Free, Step-free) sit above the answer only while restrooms are shown.
  • The partner card shows who may use it, hours, step-free access, changing table, how to get in, and when it was last checked. "It was closed" sends a report; three in a week trigger a check visit.
  • The business screens are new: opt-in with who, hours, step-free, changing table, door code, optional offer; a dashboard with people sent, busy hours, reports and a pause switch. Counts only; no visitor is tracked.
How promotion stays inside restroom results
  • The app's design rules forbid suggesting food, cafés or shops unless asked. Partners appear only when someone asks for a restroom.
  • Distance comes first. Level only breaks ties among places a similar walk away, so nobody in a hurry is sent past a closer open bathroom.
  • A partner's offer appears only on its own card. Nobody pays for rank.
Google and Apple: what we found
  • Google Places has a yes/no restroom field and wheelchairAccessibleRestroom.3 Owners set Business Profile attributes; some come from customer answers.4 The Business Profile API can write attributes for a listing a partner manages; the exact restroom attribute names are not published.9
  • Google Maps Content Partners accepts uploads of public bathrooms from approved organisations, but sends businesses back to their owners.22
  • Apple's place cards confirm only "has gender-neutral restrooms". Attributes come from partners and data providers through the Apple Business API, for approved enterprise or agency partners.5,10
  • OpenStreetMap is the only open route that carries all the fields today (toilets:access=customers, fee, hours, wheelchair, changing table). Anything added there is shared under the ODbL licence.6,8
  • Precedent: India's housing ministry supplied data for 57,000+ public toilets on Google Maps and took ownership of the listings (2019).12 Paris publishes its toilets as open data.23 We found no case of a city's restroom data reaching Apple.
The City: what the plan says
  • The plan counts about 1,100 publicly owned bathrooms, already above the law's floor of 1,060, so private partners carry the rest of the 2,120.11
  • It names two payment models: waivers of minor civil penalties for small businesses, or a direct stipend in three tiers. It says New York would need a higher stipend than London's.11,24
  • It proposes considering a pilot in a limited number of high-need corridors, reporting in 2030, and agreements that set cleaning, inspection rights, hours, term and an opt-out.11
  • It may add a bathroom question to the Small Business Services storefront survey (150,000+ storefronts every 90 days).11
  • Council Member Sandy Nurse said the plan needs "more meat on the bone"; targets move to an action plan due by the end of 2027.16
  • Other levers: Int. 1037 would study using private bathrooms for delivery workers;14 Small Business Services community grants;25 Local Law 92 of 2025 (Rita Joseph) requires updates on new restroom sites;26 the Council's "Good To Go?" report asked for an accurate public restroom map.27
  • Payment levels elsewhere: Berlin €140–365 a month;18 Essen €50–150 a month;28 London boroughs £500–1,200 a year.24
Not confirmed
  • The plan's exact release date (about 30 Oct 2026) and any budget line for a business program.
  • Restroom attribute names in Google's Business Profile API, and any restroom field in Apple Maps beyond gender-neutral.
  • Whether Apple uses OpenStreetMap business or toilet tags in New York.
  • The current MTA chair and City Planning chair.
  • Cleaning cost per bathroom visit.
