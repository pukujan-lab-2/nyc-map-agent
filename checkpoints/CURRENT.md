## In flight

**Subway trains were drawn stopping up to 50 s at nine large complexes and up to 60 s at 68 transfer stations, whether or not anything was there to wait for; in one live snapshot 13.1% of drawn stops ran over 40 s (#273, branch `feat/273-train-connection-stops`, task NMA-0069; owner, 2026-11-20: trains "usually stop for 20-40 seconds not longer", and where the transfer is straight across the platform they wait for each other).** No ordinary stop is drawn longer than 40 s now. A transfer station gets the 40 s only while a train of another service is due on the same platform, from 15 s before our train to 30 s after it; with nobody to wait for it is an ordinary 30 s stop (`src/features/transit/connections.ts`). On the same snapshot nothing is over 40 s and 63 of the 433 stops at transfer stations have a connection pulling in. The 7 pairs with the N and W at Queensboro Plaza, and Lexington Av/63 St and 7 Av on 53 St were added to the transfer list. The window and the list are judgements, not measurements, and the map still cannot invent a wait the feed does not show. Built on #267's branch, so it merges after it.

**When a subway train is held, the trains coming up behind it were still drawn running on by the timetable, and so were trains approaching a station a delay notice names (#266, branch `fix/266-train-trouble-ahead`, task NMA-0067; owner, 2026-11-20: trains are affected "during delay or if there is station trouble upstream or downstream").** The map kept a train where its vehicle report put it only when the train itself was held or its line had a notice. It now does the same for a train with a held train standing at one of its next three stops on the same platform, and for a train whose next three stops include a station an unplanned delay or detour notice names (`src/features/transit/troubledTrains.ts`). Planned work, the other direction and commuter rail are unchanged. Covered by unit tests only; it has not been watched during a real hold.

**Subway trains on the map sat too long at stations and then crossed to the next one faster than a subway can go: 12.9% of the legs drawn in one live snapshot were faster than the fastest run a car can make (#257, branch `fix/257-train-run-first`, task NMA-0065; owner, 2026-11-20: "stop way too long in the station ... and then travel very fast between stops").** Where the feed gives no departure time, the map gave each station a fixed stop of 30, 50 or 60 s and took it out of the gap to the next arrival, so the run got whatever was left. The split now goes the other way: the run gets the time a train normally needs for its distance (1.5 times the fastest possible run, calibrated on seven days of subwaydata.nyc), and the stop gets the rest, up to the old default and never under 10 s while the gap allows it. On the same snapshot the racing legs fall to 0.5%; the median stop stays 30 s. Arrival times, departures the feed supplies and commuter rail are unchanged. Not done: real stop times per station (the feed gives two estimates for the numbered lines, 17 to 27 s and about 54 s, and none for the lettered lines), the trouble mode for delays and alerts, and a parked and racing score on the tracker health page.

**The Walk Guide counted in its own steps, so a rider who steps shorter was told "you should see the sign" too early: on our Union Sq recording the rider took 296 steps where the guide counts 212 (rider-walks loop, #186, branch `feat/186-rider-step-pace`).** The guide now learns a rider's pace when they confirm the next sign and counts their steps at that pace (`src/features/walkguide/progress.ts`, tests in `pace.test.ts`). Replaying that rider's counts, the prompt comes 95 steps too early in total without it and 50 with it; the split of steps between the mezzanine signs is an estimate, the totals are measured. Checked in the built app by hand: the steps to go change from 190 to the rider's own count after one confirmed leg. Not run here: the browser test at the four sizes, because this Mac has no Playwright browser; CI runs it. Also found: the MTA's 2021 draft station data still matches today's elevator list (all 308 of its elevators are in the live feed; the live feed has 122 more). Not built: a reset from an elevator ride, because a web page cannot read the barometer; that waits for the native app.

**One browser test failed about three runs in seven on 2026-11-20 and blocked pull requests that had nothing to do with it (branch `test/offline-replay-settle`).** `e2e/offline-replay.spec.ts`, the Spanish trip card with the network off, paused a fixed second after the card's second translation request before going offline; on the busy CI machine a later request was still on its way, so a line read in English ("Walk to Grand Central-42 St").
